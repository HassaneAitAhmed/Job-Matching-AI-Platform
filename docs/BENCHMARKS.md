# Benchmarks

Generated by `python -m matching evaluate` on Python 3.13.5 (Windows AMD64). Re-run it after changing the model.

> The bundled datasets appear to be synthetic (see [DATA.md](DATA.md)). These numbers describe algorithm behaviour, not real-world hiring quality.

## 1. Planted-match recovery

A query is built from a known item (a job's own requirements, or a seeker's own profile), and we record where that item is ranked. In the *noisy* variant, sector or contract may be blank, the city may be swapped for one within 200 km, and salary, skills and experience are perturbed. This checks that the model is sensible; it does not measure real-world relevance.

## 2. Agreement with the exhaustive optimum, and speed

These queries are real items from the other side of the market, with sector, contract, education, city or salary each left blank 30% of the time. k = 10. *Optimal top-k* means the algorithm's 10 scores equal the exhaustive top 10. Times are for the algorithm only (`stats.elapsed_ms`). The exhaustive baseline is a vectorised NumPy pass, which is why it is fast on 754 / 8,124 items. A* wins on *items scored*, which is what matters when scoring is expensive or the catalogue is large.

**seeker_to_job** (n=200 queries over 754 items)

| algorithm | optimal top-k | mean score vs optimal | median ms | p95 ms | median items scored | median nodes expanded | CSP relaxed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| astar | 100% | 100.0% | 5.48 | 9.59 | 10 | 38 | - |
| greedy | 1% | 79.4% | 5.69 | 11.04 | 10 | 38 | - |
| csp | 38% | 96.8% | 4.01 | 10.20 | 35 | 62 | 93% |
| exhaustive | 100% | 100.0% | 0.50 | 0.93 | 754 | 0 | - |

**job_to_seeker** (n=200 queries over 8124 items)

| algorithm | optimal top-k | mean score vs optimal | median ms | p95 ms | median items scored | median nodes expanded | CSP relaxed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| astar | 100% | 100.0% | 11.82 | 51.01 | 16 | 71 | - |
| greedy | 0% | 72.7% | 36.08 | 84.35 | 11 | 204 | - |
| csp | 26% | 97.3% | 4.93 | 82.96 | 38 | 42 | 55% |
| exhaustive | 100% | 100.0% | 1.45 | 2.98 | 8124 | 0 | - |

The CSP is *expected* to differ from the optimum: it removes items that break a hard constraint, even when they would score well on the soft criteria.

## 3. Batch assignment: GA vs optimal

Random batches of seekers and job postings; each job accepts up to its number of `available posts`. Pair score = mean of the seeker's and the employer's perspective. Optimal = Hungarian algorithm (`scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment`). GA: population 60, 200 generations, 5 seeds per size.

| batch | method | mean % of optimal | worst % | mean ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 seekers x 8 jobs | optimal (Hungarian) | 100.0% | 100.0% | 0.1 |
| 20 seekers x 8 jobs | greedy | 99.7% | 99.0% | 0.1 |
| 20 seekers x 8 jobs | genetic algorithm | 100.0% | 99.9% | 1068.3 |
| 40 seekers x 15 jobs | optimal (Hungarian) | 100.0% | 100.0% | 0.1 |
| 40 seekers x 15 jobs | greedy | 99.2% | 98.5% | 0.5 |
